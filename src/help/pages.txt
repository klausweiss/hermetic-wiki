PAGES AND REFERENCES

  Every page shows its own blocks, then everything the rest of the notes
  say about it. Nothing has to be filed in one place: write in today's
  journal, link the topics, and each topic's page collects it.

  Pages without a file
      Every name that is linked but has no file still gets a page, showing
      only what links to it. So does every namespace above a page
      (Project X for Project X/Auth Flow), and a linked date gets a journal
      page. Markdown links to missing files do not create pages.

  Linked references
      A page lists every block on other pages that links to it, or to one
      of its blocks with ((id)). Each is shown with its breadcrumb (the
      blocks above it) and all its children. If a block and one of its
      children both link the page, only the outer block is listed. Groups
      come from journals first, newest day first, then from pages in
      alphabetical order. Links from the page to itself are not listed.

  Inherited references and tasks
      A block's children belong to everything the block links: a TODO under
      "meeting about [[auth]]" is a task of auth. A page shows its open
      tasks (TODO and DOING): those written on it and those under blocks
      that link to it. The open tasks page lists all open tasks.

  Related pages
      Pages linked together in the same block are related. A page lists
      its related pages, the most often linked together first, then the
      most recently; each expands to the blocks they share. Only links in
      the block itself count, not those of its parents or the page it is
      on.

  Namespaces
      A namespaced page links to the pages above it and lists the pages
      directly below it.

  Site-wide pages
      Home page (index.md, or the journal list), journal list (newest
      first, grouped by month, also readable as one timeline), all pages
      (implicit ones marked), open tasks, search, and the graph. Long lists
      of linked references or tasks continue on further pages, 100 per page.

  Graph
      graph.html draws every page other than journal days as a node, bigger
      the more pages link to it, and joins two pages when a block links one
      to the other or mentions both (also in journals). Click a node to
      open its page. With JavaScript, drag to pan and scroll or pinch to
      zoom; more names appear as you zoom in. Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on macOS)
      finds pages by name: Enter goes through the matches, Ctrl+Enter
      opens one, Escape ends the search. Press Ctrl+F again for the
      browser's own find.

  Search
      Over http:// (hermetic serve, or any static host), the header has a
      search box: press / or Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on macOS). It searches page
      names, block text, and property values; ignores case and accents;
      matches word forms (testing finds tested) and the start of words
      (auth finds authentication); and tolerates one typo when nothing
      else matches. Results are blocks and pages; Enter opens the full
      list on search.html.

  Reading
      Shift+click a link to open the page in the sidebar next to the
      current one (over http://). Press g g for the graph, ? for all
      keyboard shortcuts.
